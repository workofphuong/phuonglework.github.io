Picture Health

From my first week to a marketing engine

Early-stage B2B biotech. Reported to the CEO and CMO.

In three months I ran a conference launch, doubled the company's LinkedIn engagement rate, and built the automation that kept the marketing running.

Chapter 1

One week before ASCO

I joined with no oncology background, and ASCO, one of the field's biggest conferences, started a week later. I treated the week as a project: cancer basics first, then treatment classes (especially anti-angiogenics), then how tumor vascularity is measured. It cost me approximately a week to understand the baseline technical knowledge and adapt to the new working environment.

Then I managed the event across all three phases: before, during and after. The recap I posted on June 2 became one of the two posts that drove most of the summer's growth in clicks.

Chapter 2

Finding the audience, then testing the post

The team was pivoting between audience groups on LinkedIn, so I tested content angles, wording and formats, including a product post built around a video demo, to see which group most of our viewers belonged to and which formats drove growth. I built ideal customer profiles from that and from account-based research, and reported the findings to the CEO and CMO.

I analyzed past performance from data pulled from LinkedIn analytics. Several of the most-engaged posts were about events, with people in the visuals.

Across May to July 2026, the page's engagement rate doubled. A typical earlier month ran at 8%; June reached 21% and July 18%. Clicks more than doubled too: a typical earlier month had 294, and the summer averaged 646, with click-through rising from 4% to 12%. Average likes per post went from 19 in May to 30 in July, against 22 in a typical earlier month (July rests on four posts), and new followers per month rose from 20 to 25 to 34. Reach stayed flat, so the gain came from more engagement per view, not more views.

Source: LinkedIn page analytics exports, June 2025 to July 2026, organic only. A typical earlier month is the median month from June 2025 to April 2026.

Chapter 3

Making it run without me

Nobody had time to manage our presence across channels, so I audited the website, LinkedIn, one-pagers and emails over a couple of days. Then I built the baseline myself: a brand guideline, design templates and a performance report.

The repeated work was the next problem. I designed five AI workflows with Claude Co-work that produce marketing materials, periodic performance reports, outreach and a weekly digest, shared with leadership when needed. Team productivity improved by 70% and periodic marketing ran smoothly without me. I had no AI experience when I started, so I learned by building real workflows for the team.

Here is how the five fit together. They run as one loop: what we watch shapes what we write about, and what we publish shapes the next report.

Quarterly

Competitive landscape and industry trends

Tracks named competitors and industry organizations (for example Onc.Live and ASCO) through Google, websites and LinkedIn, and compares our social performance with similar companies and with our own history. Quarterly report, plus annual slides for executives.

Every Friday

Weekly digest

A scheduled task that browses industry sites, LinkedIn and newsletters by keyword list and writes an HTML summary with suggested drafts or LinkedIn actions. I review it, and send leadership a consolidated version when needed.

Often

Topic brainstorm

Starts from the digest, our library of past research and papers, the industry tracker and customer personas, and suggests what to write about next.

The rule: every suggestion has to trace back to our own research and real science. No guessing.

Whenever I post

Content drafts

Writes ready-to-post captions in our usual structure: hook, value, product, call to action. It follows the voice of each platform (LinkedIn, website, whitepaper) and our past posts, and gives several tones to choose from.

Posts go live, and their results feed the report. Top posts feed the next drafts.

Monthly

Performance report

I drop the LinkedIn analytics export in a folder. Claude cleans it and builds a short executive deck: snapshot, key metrics, best and weakest posts, what worked and next month's aims, with the detail in an appendix. About a day by hand, under 10 minutes now.
